## Customer discovery

Start with people who recently experienced the problem. Ask about actual past behavior, not whether they like the proposed product.

Useful prompts:

- Tell me about the last time you tried to do this.
- What happened, and what did you do next?
- How often does this come up? What does it cost in time, money, or risk?
- What have you already tried or paid for?
- Who else is involved in choosing or approving a solution?
- What would make you switch, and what would prevent it?

## Experiments

Run one experiment against the riskiest assumption at a time. Set the pass/fail/inconclusive threshold before collecting results.

| Assumption | Experiment | Audience / channel | Cost and duration | Pass / fail / inconclusive threshold | Result | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown | Interview or manual offer | Unknown | Unknown | Set before launch | Not run | Open |

Possible low-cost tests include targeted conversations, a manual concierge service, a clear landing page with a real call to action, a paid pilot offer, or a clickable mockup used to test a specific workflow. Choose the test based on the uncertainty; do not build a full product just to learn whether the problem exists. A failed test can also be inconclusive if too few qualified people saw the offer or the channel/test setup was invalid; record that evidence.
